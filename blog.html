Research That Expanded My Perspective

I have worked in three research labs, each with a very different lens on engineering. Together, they taught me how to think from atomic-scale interactions all the way to full-system behavior:

  • Nanoscale heat transfer — exploring thermal energy behavior at extremely small scales, where understanding conduction, radiation, and interfaces becomes critical.
  • Spectroscopic ellipsometry — an optical thin-film characterization method that analyzes polarization changes in reflected light to estimate material properties.
  • Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) — numerical simulation of fluid flow and boundary interactions to evaluate performance before expensive physical testing.

Working across these labs sharpened my ability to switch mindsets quickly: precise experimental work when data quality matters most, and large-scale modeling when design decisions depend on simulation. That flexibility is one of my strongest advantages as an engineer.

Phantom Regiment and the Performance Standard

I am a member of the Phantom Regiment Drum and Bugle Corps. I have participated every summer since 2024, and I am planning to return again in summer 2026.

Phantom Regiment is a long-standing Drum Corps International (DCI) World Class organization based in Rockford, Illinois, known for sustained competitive excellence and iconic performances. Being part of that culture has pushed me to raise my standards in rehearsal discipline, consistency, and execution under pressure.

Music is one of my biggest passions, and it is a constant part of my day. The same focus I bring to engineering analysis shows up in how I approach rehearsal reps, technique, and ensemble timing.

What Keeps Me Sharp Outside the Lab

I care about performance in every part of life, not only in coursework and projects. Outside engineering, my hobbies are where I reset, compete with myself, and keep my edge:

  • Gym training — I love bench press and I am working toward a 225 lb bench goal by summer. I do abs every day, no excuses.
  • Lower-body work — I choose leg press over squats because I prefer reducing injury risk while still training hard and consistently.
  • Baking — banana bread and cakes are two favorites, especially when I can dial in texture and flavor like an experiment.
  • Cooking — chicken-focused high-protein meal prep, bagel sandwiches, and steak are staples in my routine.
  • Gaming — I play Minecraft, Rocket League, Overwatch, and Satisfactory as a way to unwind while still enjoying strategy, focus, and competitive energy.

Whether I am chasing strength milestones, perfecting a recipe, or planning a week of high-protein meals, I approach hobbies the same way I approach engineering: show up consistently, track progress, and improve with intent.

That blend of discipline and creativity is a big part of who I am. I enjoy building momentum in every arena— in the lab, in rehearsal, in the gym, and in the kitchen.

Satisfactory especially clicks with how I think as an engineer: systems planning, throughput optimization, process layout, and iterative improvement all mirror the mindset I use in real-world technical work.

Where I’m Headed

I am building a career at the intersection of hands-on design, advanced analysis, and elite execution. My goal is to keep growing as the kind of engineer who can model a system, build the prototype, communicate the result, and perform when the stakes are high.

Long-term, I plan to pursue a master’s program in nuclear engineering with a focus on computational reactor design. I want to apply rigorous simulation, thermal-fluid analysis, and systems thinking to some of the most technically demanding energy challenges.
